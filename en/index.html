Why these ideas

The logic of the selection, in brief.

An answer increasingly replaces the click

According to 2026 data, 68% of Google searches in the US ended without a click (SparkToro), and when an AI answer sits above the results, the click-through rate of the first position is 58% lower (Ahrefs). A ranking is worth less than it used to be, while mentions and citations matter more.

Ideas: 3, 4 and 5.

See what works first

Before building anything new, there has to be one picture of the sites that already run: what gets impressions and clicks, and what costs money and returns nothing. As of autumn 2026 there is no such picture: Search Console is not connected to the infrastructure.

Ideas: 1 and 2.

Small steps instead of big projects

Each idea is sized for one person and a few dozen hours, reuses what already runs, and has a stop condition. No new subscriptions are required: paid tools are listed as alternatives, not as prerequisites.

Ideas: all seven.

What is already at hand

The ideas build on these systems. They work today, and using them as they are is enough.

Site factory and admin

A multi-tenant engine, 54 design packs, roles, a “draft, owner approval, publish” flow and an audit log.

For ideas: 5, 6 and 7, to publish pages and the tool.

domain-scout pipeline

Candidates, paid enrichment with Ahrefs API spend under control, rules, a spreadsheet and a chat bot.

For ideas: 3, to find and score sites; 2, for domain data.

HTML page publisher

Versions in R2, automatic domains and certificates, checks before publishing.

For ideas: 5, 6 and 7.

LLM pipeline

Draft, links from web search, quality check.

For ideas: 4 and 5, to run the questions and draft comparisons, always with human review.

Domain monitoring

Registration, DNS, site response and redirects checked every 15 minutes.

For idea 2: the state of every site in one score.

The systems are covered by automated tests and documentation. Hosting costs about $12 a month (Cloudflare and Render, May 2026 data).

Seven ideas in three groups

For each idea: what is done, what the team gets, when to stop, and what can be bought ready-made. Hours are estimates for one person.

Ideas 1–2about 30 h

Measurement and order

Before building anything new, see what already works and what costs money.

1One picture of all owned sites

15–20 h

Connect Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools to every owned site, add UTM tags to outgoing links to Stripo, and count clicks on buttons. Once a week, compile a digest: impressions, clicks, queries and pages, and how they change.

As of autumn 2026 Search Console is not connected to the infrastructure, and there is no common picture across the sites.

Result
A spreadsheet and a weekly digest in the team chat: which sites and pages get impressions and clicks, and which do not.
When to stop
The idea is finished when every site is connected and the digest arrives without manual work. After that, only maintenance.
Ready-made instead
The Search Console API is free; for manual review there are Looker Studio and Ahrefs Webmaster Tools. Custom code is needed only to collect data across many sites and to build the digest.

2Portfolio review: keep, merge, redirect, close

10–15 h

Before each domain renewal, combine traffic, links, cost and site state into one score and suggest a decision. The idea builds on the data from idea 1 and on the domain monitoring that already runs.

Every domain is a renewal cost and maintenance work. Some sites may not be useful, and only data shows which.

Result
A renewal calendar with a recommendation for each domain and an estimate of the savings.
When to stop
A decision on each domain is made before its renewal date. If recommendations are regularly rejected, the scoring rules are revised.
Ready-made instead
There is no ready-made product for a portfolio like this: it is a spreadsheet and rules on top of the data from idea 1.
Ideas 3–4about 42 h

Links and mentions

Where people and AI assistants mention the brand, and where they could.

3Link radar: who links to competitors but not to Stripo

25–30 h

Through the Ahrefs API, within the shared limit, find sites that link to competitors but not to Stripo. Separately, find mentions of Stripo without a link and lost links. Candidates are scored on relevance, traffic and spam signals the same way as in domain-scout: candidates, budgeted enrichment, rules, review, spreadsheet, chat bot.

The API spend is calculated in advance, before a run.

Result
A weekly list of sites for guest posts and link reclamation, with the reason each was chosen.
When to stop
If the specialist approves fewer than a third of the list, the rules are revised; if it is still under a third afterwards, the idea is stopped.
Ready-made instead
Ahrefs Link Intersect and Semrush Backlink Gap cover the first pass of the search; Pitchbox and BuzzStream cover outreach. Custom code is for scoring by the team’s own rules and for the link to the spreadsheet and bot.

4Visibility in AI answers

12–16 h

Once a week, run a fixed set of questions (“best email editor”, “embeddable email editor”, “Stripo alternatives” and so on) through AI assistants and AI-powered search. Record whether Stripo is mentioned, which sources are cited and which competitors are named.

The metric is the share of answers that mention the brand across several runs, not a “position”: an assistant rarely repeats the same list of brands.

Result
A weekly digest: share of presence, cited sources and competitors. The list of sources becomes the target list for idea 3.
When to stop
The idea is finished when a baseline share is measured and the digest shows week-to-week change.
Ready-made instead
Ahrefs Brand Radar, Semrush AI Toolkit, Peec AI, Otterly, Profound. If a subscription is acceptable, buying is easier; if not, a light version on the API takes a few hours.

Basis: brand mentions correlate with visibility in AI answers more strongly than links do, 0.66–0.71 against 0.22 (Ahrefs); an assistant rarely repeats the same list of brands (Search Engine Land).

Ideas 5–7about 72 h

Pages and tools

Material that is worth linking to and worth citing.

5Honest comparisons on owned sites

20–28 h

Rewrite several review pages so they can be cited: state openly who runs the site, the criteria, the date of the check and the sources of prices; compare on facts, including Stripo’s weak points. Add markup and an update date, and once a month re-check prices and competitors’ claims.

Competitors publish their own “Stripo alternatives” pages. An honest comparison is a way to be present for the same queries.

Result
Three to five “Stripo and …” and “alternatives to …” pages with an open methodology.
When to stop
If the pages get no impressions or citations (by the data from ideas 1 and 4), no new ones are added.
Ready-made instead
No tool is needed: this is content. The LLM pipeline drafts, and a person checks facts and prices.

Basis: comparisons are the format assistants cite most often (HubSpot), while self-promotional lists are cited 65% less (Texta). Competitors’ alternatives pages: Topol, Unlayer.

6Template pages with unique data

28–36 h

Add to the gallery pages what copies lack: HTML weight and the risk of Gmail clipping, accessibility checks (alt text, language, contrast), a color palette and a list of blocks. The pipeline collects this at publication time from the HTML exported through the Stripo API. Start with one gallery and compare it with the rest by the data from idea 1.

As of autumn 2026 a template page is short (about 170 words) and largely repeats the page on stripo.email.

Result
Pages that differ from the copies and are not repeated on other sites, plus a shared page template for all galleries.
When to stop
If the pages with data do not get more impressions than the old ones once indexed, the other galleries are not reworked.
Ready-made instead
HTML checks are custom code. Rendering in real email clients is Litmus or Email on Acid (paid) and is outside this idea.

Basis: Google’s policies target mass-produced content with no added value (Google).

7One free tool

14–18 h

A small in-browser tool for email authors, for example a check of email weight and the risk of Gmail clipping, or a preview of subject line and preheader. It lives on its own domain, needs no sign-up and processes nothing on a server.

The hypothesis: one useful tool earns links and citations better than one more article. It has to be tested on one tool, not five.

Result
One working tool on Cloudflare Pages and a list of places where it is worth mentioning.
When to stop
If after several weeks the tool has earned no links and no repeat visits, no more are built.
Ready-made instead
Email-sending and email-testing services already offer similar free tools; the point is to pick a niche where there is no strong one.

How the work on each idea runs

Brief

One page per idea: what is done, how many hours, how we will know it worked.

The lead sees: the goal, the limits and the hours, before work starts.

Minimum version

A result within the stated hours. Everything lives in the team’s repository, with documentation.

The lead sees: a working result that can be opened and checked.

Measurement

The metric from idea 1, or a manual check until it exists.

The lead sees: numbers, not impressions.

Decision

Continue, change or stop. Unused hours are returned.

The lead sees: one question, yes or no.

What is needed from the SEO lead

Choice
Which ideas to take and in what order. The ideas are independent except for two links: idea 2 builds on the data from idea 1, and idea 3 uses the list of sources from idea 4.
Hours
A limit of hours for each chosen idea. Unused hours are not spent.
Access, optional
Read access to Search Console for the main site, so it can be compared with the others. Without it the ideas work on the owned sites.
Not needed
Involvement of other teams: product, sales, legal, partnerships. New subscriptions are needed only if someone wants to replace a custom version with a ready-made one.
